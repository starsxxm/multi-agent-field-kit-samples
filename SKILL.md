# Demand Evidence Mining

Build the evidence file before you build the product. AI is good at synthesis and scoring; it is bad at inventing what the real world lacks. Every claim in the file must trace to a real person's own words.

## What counts as one piece of evidence

A single entry is only valid if it has all five parts:

- The person's exact words (copy-paste, never paraphrase)
- A link to the source (post, thread, review page)
- A date — prefer the last 12 months
- The workaround they use today
- A payment signal: they mention paying now, paying before, or being willing to pay

An entry missing the quote or the link is a note, not evidence. Vendor blogs, industry reports, and "everyone hates X" think-pieces count as zero.

## Where to mine

- Community search with intent phrases: `site:reddit.com "alternative to [competitor]"`, `"frustrated with [category]"`, `"is there a tool that [job]"`, `"how do you handle [task]"`
- Ask-style threads (Ask HN, forum Q&A) — the comments asking for missing features are demand statements
- Competitor 1–3 star reviews — reviewers describe the exact job the product fails at
- The niche's own forums — slower, but the language is unfiltered

## The pass bar

- Collect 20+ entries for one candidate direction before judging it
- At least 5 entries must carry a clear payment signal
- Below the bar: keep mining or drop the direction. Do not lower the bar because the idea feels good

## Common failure modes

- Counting complaints as demand (a complaint about price is not a request for your product)
- Counting upvotes/likes as payment signals
- Mining only sources that agree with the idea — deliberately search for "I tried X and went back to spreadsheets" style posts too
